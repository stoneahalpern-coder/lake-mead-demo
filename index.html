Lake Mead Water Bank — can one-year water trades hold the lake above 1,020 ft?

The policy question: in a dry stretch, can voluntary one-year compensated water trades among California, Arizona, Nevada, Mexico, and the Tribal Nations of the Lower Basin keep Lake Mead above the 1,020 ft protection elevation — and who ends up paying for the water that does it?

Who would use the answer: J.B. Hamby, Chairman of the Colorado River Board of California and California’s Colorado River Commissioner — the state’s lead negotiator in post-2026 Colorado River operations, where compensated conservation and interstate water deals are the live instruments.

Try it in two minutes: press Start session below, pick an inflow, and settle one year — play it yourself on the dashboard or let an AI play all five parties from their documented public positions. The history log then shows who traded, at what price, and where a stated hard line broke. It is a browser adaptation of David Rosenberg & Anabelle Myers' published Lake Mead model ("Divide Reservoir Inflow," Utah State University); 301 Tech Policy project artifact.

How a year works

The model's three-step loop (Model Guide, Introduction). All volumes in million acre-feet (maf); prices in $ per acre-foot.

A. Divide reservoir inflowAn inflow scenario is entered. Reclamation receives a first block exactly offsetting its evaporation share (this holds the protection elevation). The rest is divided among users by shares derived from the 2024 Lower Basin Alternative shortage splits, with Tribal Nations' settled rights taken off the top.
B. Subtract evaporationLake evaporation (6.0 ft/yr × surface area at the current elevation, official USBR area-capacity tables) is charged to each account in proportion to its balance.
C. Trade, withdraw, conserveEach account may buy/sell water for one year at an agreed $/af (compensation is computed), then withdraws within its available water. Whatever is left is banked for future years. Trades must net to zero.

The Mulholland negotiation simulator

The code behind this project, as delivered by the Mulholland team (USC Marshall), November 2026.

Thirteen AI agents play the parties in the Colorado River water dispute and negotiate allocations over several rounds. A plain Python rule engine checks every proposal against the legal limits and clamps any number that breaks them, so the AI cannot argue its way past water law. After many runs, a statistics module shows where agreement tends to land and who refuses to move.

How a run works

  1. Each round, every agent proposes an allocation, a conservation commitment, and a shortage stance in a fixed JSON format.
  2. The rule engine accepts or clamps each proposal, then the engine totals the seven states' claims against the 13.0 MAF of available water and feeds the gap into the next round.
  3. Across many runs, the analyzer reports the agreement range for each party, stable coalitions, and whether the totals ever fit the water that exists.

def validate_scenario(config: dict) -> list[str]:
    """
    Validates the scenario config before running anything.
    Returns a list of error strings — empty list means all good.
    """
    errors = []

    # Check required top-level keys
    for key in ["scenario", "supply_conditions", "simulation_parameters", "active_agents"]:
        if key not in config:
            errors.append(f"Missing required field: '{key}'")

    if errors:
        return errors   # Return early — can't validate further

    # Check all active agents exist
    for agent in config.get("active_agents", []):
        if agent not in ALL_MANDATES:
            errors.append(
                f"Unknown agent: '{agent}'. "
                f"Valid agents: {list(ALL_MANDATES.keys())}"
            )

    # Check supply makes sense
    supply = config["supply_conditions"].get("available_supply_maf", 0)
    if not (5.0 <= supply <= 20.0):
        errors.append(
            f"available_supply_maf={supply} is outside realistic range (5–20 MAF)."
        )

    # Check simulation parameters
    params = config["simulation_parameters"]
    n_sims   = params.get("num_simulations", 0)
    n_rounds = params.get("num_rounds", 0)
    if not (1 <= n_sims <= 1000):
        errors.append(f"num_simulations={n_sims} must be between 1 and 1000.")
    if not (1 <= n_rounds <= 10):
        errors.append(f"num_rounds={n_rounds} must be between 1 and 10.")

    # Check mandate overrides reference valid agents and valid fields
    for agent, overrides in config.get("mandate_overrides", {}).items():
        if agent.startswith("_comment"):
            continue
        if agent not in ALL_MANDATES:
            errors.append(f"mandate_overrides references unknown agent: '{agent}'")
            continue
        valid_fields = {"position", "negotiation_style", "red_lines", "authority",
                        "priority", "dependencies"}
        for field in overrides:
            if field not in valid_fields:
                errors.append(
                    f"mandate_overrides['{agent}'] has unknown field '{field}'. "
                    f"Valid: {valid_fields}"
                )

    return errors


# ── Mandate override application ──────────────────────────────────────────────

def apply_mandate_overrides(config: dict):
    """
    Applies mandate_overrides from the config to ALL_MANDATES in-place.
    Called before any agents are instantiated.

    This is how the client customises agent behaviour without
    editing agents.py directly.
    """
    overrides = config.get("mandate_overrides", {})
    applied   = []

    for agent, fields in overrides.items():
        if agent.startswith("_comment"):
            continue
        if agent not in ALL_MANDATES:
            continue

        for field, value in fields.items():
            ALL_MANDATES[agent][field] = value
            applied.append(f"{agent}.{field}")

    if applied:
        print(f"  Mandate overrides applied: {', '.join(applied)}")

class _Tee:
    """
    Mirrors everything written to a stream (stdout/stderr) into a file as well,
    so the FULL terminal output is saved verbatim. Flushed on every write, so
    the file lands each round as it prints — no buffering until the end and no
    change to what's shown on screen.
    """
    def __init__(self, stream, file_handle):
        self._stream = stream
        self._file   = file_handle

    def write(self, data):
        self._stream.write(data)
        self._file.write(data)
        self._stream.flush()
        self._file.flush()
        return len(data)

    def flush(self):
        self._stream.flush()
        self._file.flush()

    def __getattr__(self, name):
        # Delegate everything else (encoding, isatty, fileno, …) to the stream.
        return getattr(self._stream, name)

    # ── Resume from an interrupted run, if there is one ───────────────────────
    # A full run is NUM_SIMULATIONS × NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM × 13 LLM calls (hours

    # checkpoint from a matching config exists, pick up where it stopped.
    all_results  = []
    resume_state = None

    checkpoint = load_checkpoint()
    if checkpoint:
        if checkpoint_is_compatible(checkpoint, NUM_SIMULATIONS, NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM):
            all_results  = checkpoint.get("completed_simulations", [])
            resume_state = checkpoint.get("in_progress")
            done = len(all_results)
            print(f"  ↻ Found checkpoint — {done} of {NUM_SIMULATIONS} simulation(s) "
                  f"already complete. Resuming.\n")
        else:
            # Config changed since the checkpoint was written. Resuming would
            # blend results from two different experiment configurations into
            # one output file, which is worse than redoing the work because the
            # mismatch would be invisible in the results.
            print("  [WARNING] Existing checkpoint was written under a different "
                  "NUM_SIMULATIONS/NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM config — ignoring it and "
                  "starting fresh.\n")
            clear_checkpoint()

            "config": {
                "num_simulations":    NUM_SIMULATIONS,
                "num_rounds_per_sim": NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM,
            },
            "completed_simulations": all_results,
            "in_progress": {
                "simulation_id":      sim_id,
                "next_round":         round_num + 1,
                "simulation_results": simulation_results,
                "agent_states":       agent_states,
            },

        "simulations": NUM_SIMULATIONS,
        "rounds_each": NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM,

            # when the previous run died; every later simulation starts clean.

                resume_state and resume_state.get("simulation_id") == sim_id

            resume_state = None

                resume_state      = sim_resume,

                    "num_simulations":    NUM_SIMULATIONS,

def detect_gpu_dtype_and_backend() -> tuple[str, str | None]:
    """
    Picks a dtype + attention backend that actually work on whatever GPU is
    present, instead of assuming the A100-class hardware this config was
    originally written against.

    - bfloat16 (vLLM's default for Qwen2.5) requires Ampere or newer
      (compute capability >= 8.0). V100 is Volta (7.0) and will fail to
      load the model in bf16, so pre-Ampere cards get float16 instead.
    - vLLM's FlashAttention-2 backend also requires Ampere+ (SM80). On
      older cards it needs to be pointed at the XFormers backend instead,
      or the server fails at attention-kernel build time.

    Falls back to bfloat16 / vLLM's own default backend selection if torch
    or CUDA aren't available at import time (e.g. dry-running this file off
    the GPU node) — vLLM will do its own device check at launch anyway.

    Also fails fast (RuntimeError) if the installed `torch` build has no
    compiled kernels at all for this GPU's compute capability. This can't be
    worked around with --dtype/--attention-backend flags — it means the
    wheel itself was built without this architecture's kernels (e.g. recent
    torch/vllm releases dropped sm_70/Volta/V100 support outright). Without
    this check, that failure only surfaces ~60-90s later as a cryptic
    "CUDA error: no kernel image is available for execution on the device"
    deep inside the vLLM subprocess, after weights have already downloaded.
    """
    try:
        import torch

        if not torch.cuda.is_available():
            return "bfloat16", None

        major, minor = torch.cuda.get_device_capability(0)
        name = torch.cuda.get_device_name(0)
        cc_str = f"{major}.{minor}"

        # torch.cuda.get_arch_list() returns strings like ["sm_70", "sm_75",
        # "sm_80", "sm_86", "sm_90", "sm_100"] — the exact CUDA architectures
        # this torch build was compiled with kernels for. Parse "sm_XY" into
        # (major, minor): two-digit codes split 1+1 (e.g. "75" -> 7.5),
        # three-digit codes (Blackwell+) split 2+1 (e.g. "100" -> 10.0).
        def parse_sm(arch: str) -> tuple[int, int] | None:
            digits = arch.removeprefix("sm_").removesuffix("a")
            if not digits.isdigit():
                return None
            return (int(digits[:-1]), int(digits[-1]))

        supported_ccs = {
            parsed for arch in torch.cuda.get_arch_list()
            if (parsed := parse_sm(arch)) is not None
        }

        if supported_ccs and (major, minor) not in supported_ccs:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"Installed torch=={torch.__version__} has no compiled CUDA kernels "
                f"for {name} (compute capability {cc_str}). This build only supports: "
                f"{sorted(supported_ccs)}. This is NOT fixable with --dtype or "
                f"VLLM_ATTENTION_BACKEND flags — the kernels simply aren't in this "
                f"wheel. Reinstall the pinned torch/vllm versions from "
                f"requirements.txt (`pip install -r requirements.txt`), which target "
                f"CUDA 12.1 wheels that still include this architecture. See "
                f"VLLM_GPU_GUIDE.md for the full explanation."
            )

        if major < 8:
            print(f"[vllm] Detected pre-Ampere GPU ({name}, compute capability "
                  f"{cc_str}) — using float16 + XFormers attention backend "
                  f"instead of the bfloat16/FlashAttention defaults, which "
                  f"require Ampere (A100) or newer.")
            return "float16", "XFORMERS"
        return "bfloat16", None
    except RuntimeError:
        raise
    except Exception:
        return "bfloat16", None


OSS_DTYPE, OSS_ATTENTION_BACKEND = detect_gpu_dtype_and_backend()


def find_bundled_cuda_home() -> str | None:
    """
    Some of vLLM's own dependencies (e.g. nvidia-cuda-nvcc, pulled in via
    torch) bundle a working `nvcc` directly inside this Python environment's
    site-packages — at e.g. .../site-packages/nvidia/cu13/bin/nvcc. HPC
    compute nodes commonly have no system-wide CUDA toolkit on PATH, but this
    pip-installed copy is enough for vLLM's JIT-compiled kernels (e.g.
    FlashInfer's sampling kernel) to build successfully.

    Searches the *current* environment (works for any venv/conda env, any
    CUDA version — no hardcoded username or version number) and returns the
    CUDA_HOME-style directory (the parent of bin/nvcc), or None if not found.
    """
    site_packages = sysconfig.get_paths()["purelib"]
    matches = glob.glob(os.path.join(site_packages, "nvidia", "cu*", "bin", "nvcc"))
    if not matches:
        return None
    nvcc_path = matches[0]
    return os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(nvcc_path))  # strip off /bin/nvcc

class VLLMServerManager:
    """
    Owns the vLLM server subprocess's entire lifecycle. Everything here stays
    on this machine — 127.0.0.1 only, no external network exposure, no key
    needed. Use as a context manager so the model is guaranteed to start
    before simulations run and guaranteed to be killed afterward — including
    on error/interrupt.

        with VLLMServerManager() as client:
            ... run simulations using `client` (an openai.OpenAI instance) ...
        # server process is already terminated here
    """

    def __init__(self):
        self.proc = None
        self.client = None
        self._log_file = None

    def _check_port_free(self) -> None:
        """
        Fails fast if something already holds VLLM_PORT.

        An interrupted run (SIGKILL, node preemption, a hard Ctrl-C that skips
        __exit__) can orphan a vLLM server that keeps the port — and its GPU
        memory — indefinitely. Without this check the new server starts anyway,
        dies on "OSError: [Errno 98] Address already in use" *inside the
        subprocess*, and the parent just reports a generic "exited early" after
        burning the full startup wait — leaving you to dig the real one-line
        cause out of vllm_server.log. Checking here turns that into an instant,
        actionable error.

        We deliberately do NOT auto-kill the port holder: it may be a server
        from a different job you're intentionally running, and silently killing
        another process (and its GPU allocation) is not a decision this code
        should make for you.
        """
        with socket.socket(socket.AF_INET, socket.SOCK_STREAM) as s:
            # SO_REUSEADDR mirrors what a server does, so this bind test
            # reflects whether vLLM itself could actually bind the port.
            s.setsockopt(socket.SOL_SOCKET, socket.SO_REUSEADDR, 1)
            try:
                s.bind((VLLM_HOST, VLLM_PORT))
            except OSError:
                raise RuntimeError(
                    f"Port {VLLM_PORT} on {VLLM_HOST} is already in use — most likely an "
                    f"orphaned vLLM server from a previous run that was killed before it "
                    f"could shut down cleanly.\n\n"
                    f"  See what's holding it:  lsof -i :{VLLM_PORT}\n"
                    f"  Kill just that process: lsof -ti :{VLLM_PORT} | xargs -r kill -9\n"
                    f"  Kill all your servers:  pkill -9 -u $USER -f 'vllm serve'\n\n"
                    f"Or set VLLM_PORT to a free port to run alongside it "
                    f"(note: a second 7B server needs its own ~15GB of GPU memory)."
                ) from None

    def __enter__(self) -> OpenAI:
        # Cheap preflight before the expensive work (weight check, model load) —
        # a busy port makes everything after it pointless.
        self._check_port_free()

        # Pre-warm the cache and print explicit "did we download or reuse
        # cache" feedback. We deliberately do NOT hand the resolved local
        # snapshot path to `vllm serve` below — passing a raw local
        # directory takes a less-common code path inside vLLM's model
        # resolution and was observed re-triggering a second full download
        # of the same weights. Passing the plain repo id instead lets vLLM
        # do its own (well-tested) HF cache lookup, which — since HF_HOME is
        # inherited by the subprocess — hits the exact same cache we just
        # warmed, instantly, with no redundant network transfer.
        ensure_weights_downloaded()

        # Invoke the `vllm` console script directly rather than `python -m
        # vllm.entrypoints.cli.main` — that internal module path is NOT
        # stable across vLLM releases (it was introduced in a later CLI
        # refactor; vllm==0.6.x, for example, doesn't have it at all and
        # raises ModuleNotFoundError). "vllm serve <model> [flags]" is the
        # publicly documented, version-stable entrypoint — pip always
        # installs it as a console script next to the interpreter it was
        # installed for, so resolving it relative to sys.executable (rather
        # than trusting PATH, which pip --user installs often aren't on)
        # works the same whether you're in a venv, conda env, or --user
        # install.
        vllm_bin = os.path.join(os.path.dirname(sys.executable), "vllm")
        if not os.path.exists(vllm_bin):
            vllm_bin = "vllm"  # fall back to PATH lookup as a last resort

        cmd = [
            vllm_bin, "serve", OSS_MODEL_PATH,
            "--host", VLLM_HOST,
            "--port", str(VLLM_PORT),
            "--tensor-parallel-size", str(TENSOR_PARALLEL_SIZE),
            "--gpu-memory-utilization", str(GPU_MEMORY_UTILIZATION),
            "--max-model-len", str(MAX_MODEL_LEN),
            "--dtype", OSS_DTYPE,
            # By default vllm==0.6.x runs the engine in a separate subprocess
            # from the API server and talks to it over ZeroMQ IPC (Unix
            # domain sockets, via a temp-dir socket file). On HPC clusters
            # where the temp dir resolves onto a network/parallel filesystem
            # (e.g. Lustre-backed scratch, common on CARC-style clusters),
            # that socket transport isn't supported and every RPC call fails
            # with "zmq.error.ZMQError: Operation not supported" (ENOTSUP) —
            # even after the model has already loaded onto the GPU fine.
            # Disabling frontend multiprocessing runs the engine in-process
            # with the API server instead, sidestepping ZMQ/IPC entirely.
            # Harmless here: this server only ever handles one local client
            # (this repo's own simulations) at a time, so the throughput
            # vLLM normally gains from a separate engine process doesn't
            # matter.
            "--disable-frontend-multiprocessing",
            # Skip CUDA graph capture. Costs a little per-token decode
            # latency, but cuts startup time and peak memory during engine
            # init — a good trade for this repo's batch-simulation workload,
            # which isn't latency-sensitive per token.
            "--enforce-eager",
            # vLLM defaults to swap_space=4 (GiB) of CPU-side KV cache, which
            # it allocates as *pinned* host memory right after it logs
            # "# GPU blocks / # CPU blocks". Pinned pages can't be swapped
            # out, so on an allocation with a tight host-RAM cgroup limit
            # (typical of CARC interactive GPU sessions) that single ~4.3GB
            # request is enough for the OOM killer to SIGKILL the process —
            # exit code -9, no Python traceback, right after the model itself
            # loaded onto the GPU fine. This swap space only matters for
            # preemption under heavy concurrent load; this server handles one
            # local client at a time, so it's dead weight here.
            "--swap-space", str(SWAP_SPACE_GB),
        ]

        env = os.environ.copy()

        # Force the attention backend on pre-Ampere GPUs (e.g. V100), where
        # vLLM's default FlashAttention-2 backend can't build/run at all.
        if OSS_ATTENTION_BACKEND:
            env["VLLM_ATTENTION_BACKEND"] = OSS_ATTENTION_BACKEND

        # vLLM's FlashInfer sampling path JIT-compiles a CUDA kernel on first
        # use. FlashInfer's own bundled CCCL/libcudacxx headers were observed
        # to be incompatible with CUDA 13.x nvcc on this environment ("CUDA
        # compiler and CUDA toolkit headers are incompatible" — an upstream
        # FlashInfer/CUDA-13 version gap, not something fixable by pointing
        # at a different nvcc). Force-disable the FlashInfer sampler so vLLM
        # falls back to its regular PyTorch-native sampler instead — no JIT
        # compilation involved, avoids this whole class of failure. This is
        # a direct assignment (not setdefault) so it wins even if a CARC
        # module/profile has already set this to something else.
        env["VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_SAMPLER"] = "0"

        # Separately: some *other* JIT-compiled kernel elsewhere in vLLM
        # might still need nvcc and not hit the FlashInfer/CCCL incompatibility
        # above, so keep pointing CUDA_HOME at the pip-bundled nvcc as a
        # general-purpose fallback (harmless if unused).
        cuda_home = find_bundled_cuda_home()
        if cuda_home:
            print(f"[vllm] Found bundled CUDA toolkit (nvcc) at {cuda_home}")
            env.setdefault("CUDA_HOME", cuda_home)
            env["PATH"] = os.path.join(cuda_home, "bin") + os.pathsep + env.get("PATH", "")
        else:
            print("[vllm] WARNING: no local nvcc found in this environment — "
                  "other JIT-compiled kernels may fail to build. "
                  "If startup fails with an nvcc error, try `module load cuda` before running.")

        print(f"[vllm] Starting local server: {' '.join(cmd)}")
        self._log_file = open("vllm_server.log", "w")
        self.proc = subprocess.Popen(cmd, stdout=self._log_file, stderr=subprocess.STDOUT, env=env)

        self._wait_until_healthy()
        print(f"[vllm] Server healthy at {VLLM_BASE_URL}")

        self.client = OpenAI(base_url=VLLM_BASE_URL, api_key=PLACEHOLDER_KEY)
        return self.client

    def _wait_until_healthy(self):
        deadline = time.time() + STARTUP_TIMEOUT_SEC
        last_progress_print = 0
        while time.time() < deadline:
            if self.proc.poll() is not None:
                raise RuntimeError(
                    f"vLLM server exited early (code {self.proc.returncode}) — see vllm_server.log"
                )
            try:
                r = requests.get(f"http://{VLLM_HOST}:{VLLM_PORT}/health", timeout=2)
                if r.status_code == 200:
                    return
            except requests.exceptions.RequestException:
                pass

            elapsed = time.time() - (deadline - STARTUP_TIMEOUT_SEC)
            if elapsed - last_progress_print >= 30:
                print(f"[vllm] Still waiting for server to become healthy "
                      f"({int(elapsed)}s elapsed) — tail vllm_server.log for details...")
                last_progress_print = elapsed

            time.sleep(2)
        raise TimeoutError(
            f"vLLM server did not become healthy within {STARTUP_TIMEOUT_SEC}s — "
            f"check vllm_server.log for what it was doing (model loading, CUDA graph "
            f"capture, and kernel compilation on a first run can genuinely take several "
            f"minutes for a 7B model)."
        )

    def __exit__(self, exc_type, exc_val, exc_tb):
        if self.proc and self.proc.poll() is None:
            print("[vllm] Stopping local server...")
            self.proc.terminate()
            try:
                self.proc.wait(timeout=15)
            except subprocess.TimeoutExpired:
                self.proc.kill()  # hard-kill if it won't shut down cleanly
            print("[vllm] Server stopped.")
        if self._log_file:
            self._log_file.close()
        return False  # never swallow exceptions from the `with` block

    def _trim_history(self) -> None:
        """
        Slides a fixed-width window over conversation_history: the agent always
        sees its HISTORY_WINDOW_ROUNDS most recent *past* rounds, plus the round
        it's answering right now. The window is relative to the current round,
        never anchored to specific ones — at HISTORY_WINDOW_ROUNDS=3:

            generating round  6  ->  sees rounds  3, 4, 5
            generating round  7  ->  sees rounds  4, 5, 6
            generating round 42  ->  sees rounds 39, 40, 41
            generating round 100 ->  sees rounds 97, 98, 99

        Why bound it at all: every round appends a (user, assistant) pair, and
        from round 2 on each user message embeds all 12 other agents' proposals
        — roughly 1.2k tokens per round. Left to grow, the prompt crosses the
        server's --max-model-len (8192) at ~round 6-7 and every later call fails
        with a 400 "maximum context length exceeded". With NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM at
        100, no context size fixes this on its own; a bigger window only moves
        the wall. A sliding window makes cost per round constant, so round 100
        is exactly as affordable as round 6.

        What is NOT lost: every other agent's *current* proposals are rebuilt
        into each round's live user message (sent for that call only, never
        stored), so the agent always sees the full live negotiation state. Only
        a compact record of its OWN past rounds lives in history — the bulky
        12-peer table is never replayed, which is what keeps cost per round flat.

        Each stored round is a (memory-note user, compact-reply assistant) pair
        = 2 messages. We keep the most recent HISTORY_WINDOW_ROUNDS pairs, then
        ensure the window opens on a `user` message so the chat API sees strict
        user/assistant alternation after the system prompt.
        """
        max_messages = max(2, HISTORY_WINDOW_ROUNDS * 2)
        if len(self.conversation_history) > max_messages:
            self.conversation_history = self.conversation_history[-max_messages:]
        # Guarantee the window opens on a user turn (a leading assistant message
        # right after the system prompt is invalid/odd for the chat template).
        if self.conversation_history and self.conversation_history[0].get("role") != "user":
            self.conversation_history = self.conversation_history[1:]

    def get_state(self) -> dict:
        """Serialises this agent's memory for checkpointing (see checkpoint.py)."""
        return {
            "conversation_history": self.conversation_history,
            "proposal_history":     self.proposal_history,
        }

    def load_state(self, state: dict) -> None:
        """Restores memory saved by get_state(), so a resumed agent keeps its context."""
        self.conversation_history = state.get("conversation_history", [])
        self.proposal_history     = state.get("proposal_history", [])

    @staticmethod
    def _clip(text, limit: int = 160) -> str:
        """
        Caps a free-text field so the running prompt can't grow unbounded. The
        sliding history window bounds the NUMBER of remembered rounds, but each
        round embeds 12 peers' text fields (and stores this agent's own reply);
        as agents negotiate, that prose grows and eventually blows past the
        server's --max-model-len. Clipping each field keeps per-round cost flat.
        """
        s = "" if text is None else str(text)
        return s if len(s) <= limit else s[: limit - 1].rstrip() + "…"

    def _build_pressure_text(self, round_number: int, basin_context: dict | None) -> str:
        """
        Turns the live basin numbers into an in-prompt scarcity + deadline signal.
        This is the forcing function that makes agents actually converge: they can
        see the shortfall they must collectively close, and the round it's due.
        Returns "" when no context is available (keeps behavior safe/optional).
        """
        if not basin_context:
            return ""

        supply       = basin_context.get("supply_maf")
        total_rounds = basin_context.get("total_rounds")
        claimed      = basin_context.get("last_claimed_maf")
        deficit      = basin_context.get("last_deficit_maf")

        # Round 1 (or no prior totals yet): frame the scarcity up front.
        if claimed is None or deficit is None:
            if supply is None:
                return ""
            return (
                f"\n\nSCARCITY CONTEXT — The seven basin states can realistically expect "
                f"only about {supply:.2f} MAF this year, far below the 15.0 MAF paper "
                f"compact total. A durable agreement will require shared cuts, so state "
                f"an opening position you can actually move from."
            )

        rounds_left = None
        if total_rounds is not None:
            rounds_left = max(0, total_rounds - round_number)

        deadline = f"This is round {round_number}"
        if total_rounds is not None:
            deadline += f" of {total_rounds}"
            if rounds_left is not None:
                deadline += f" — {rounds_left} round(s) left to close the gap"
        deadline += "."

        if deficit > 0.05:
            return (
                f"\nSCARCITY CHECK — Last round the seven basin states together claimed "
                f"{claimed:.2f} MAF, but only about {supply:.2f} MAF is physically "
                f"available: a shortfall of {deficit:.2f} MAF the group MUST close. "
                f"{deadline} If the gap is not closed by the final round, every party "
                f"defaults to uncontrolled shortage and litigation — worse for you than "
                f"a negotiated cut. Move a negotiable position toward closing it this "
                f"round without crossing your red lines."
            )
        # Gap effectively closed — hold the feasible deal, don't re-open it.
        return (
            f"\nSCARCITY CHECK — Last round total state claims were {claimed:.2f} MAF "
            f"against {supply:.2f} MAF available: the shortfall is essentially closed. "
            f"{deadline} Lock in this progress and hold a feasible agreement rather than "
            f"re-opening demands."
        )

    def _exhaustion_note(self) -> str:
        """
        If this agent has already exhausted its concession levers (it is sitting at
        the 50% conservation cap), tell it to HOLD. Otherwise the convergence
        pressure keeps nudging it to conserve *more*, it re-reads its own capped
        value and bumps it past 50%, and the checker clamps it again — a runaway
        ratchet (55→60→65…) with no brake. This stops the overshoot at the source.
        """
        if not self.proposal_history:
            return ""
        prev      = self.proposal_history[-1]["proposal"]
        prev_cons = float(prev.get("conservation_commitment_pct", 0) or 0)
        if prev_cons >= 49.5:
            return (
                "\nLIMIT REACHED — You are already at the maximum implementable "
                "conservation of 50%. Do NOT raise conservation above 50% (it will be "
                "rejected). You have exhausted this lever; hold your position and rely "
                "on reciprocal moves from others rather than offering more yourself."
            )
        return ""

    def _compute_conviction(self, allocation: float, conservation: float) -> float:
        """
        Derived 0–1 rigidity/conviction signal, computed from OBSERVABLE behaviour
        only — never self-reported by the model (LLM self-confidence is uncalibrated).

          high  → sitting at a hard limit and not moving (a wall)
          low   → actively flexing round-over-round (a dealmaker)

        Ingredients: how much of its legal entitlement it is still claiming, and how
        little it moved since last round (allocation + conservation deltas).
        """
        conservation = max(0.0, min(float(conservation), 50.0))
        prev = self.proposal_history[-1]["proposal"] if self.proposal_history else None

        if prev is not None:
            prev_alloc = float(prev.get("proposed_allocation_maf", allocation))
            prev_cons  = max(0.0, min(float(prev.get("conservation_commitment_pct", conservation) or 0), 50.0))
            alloc_delta = abs(allocation - prev_alloc) / self.ceiling if self.ceiling > 0 else 0.0
            cons_delta  = abs(conservation - prev_cons) / 50.0
            stillness   = 1.0 - min(alloc_delta + cons_delta, 1.0)
        else:
            stillness = 0.5   # round 1 — no prior move to measure; neutral

        if self.ceiling > 0:
            holding    = min(allocation / self.ceiling, 1.0)   # share of entitlement still claimed
            conviction = 0.5 * holding + 0.5 * stillness
        else:
            # Non-consumptive actors (BOR, Environmental) hold via posture, not water.
            conviction = stillness

        return round(max(0.0, min(conviction, 1.0)), 3)

    def generate_proposal(
        self,
        round_number: int,
        other_proposals: list[dict],
        basin_context: dict | None = None,
    ) -> dict:
        """
        Calls GPT to generate a negotiation proposal for this round.

        Round 1 : Opening position — no prior context.
        Round 2+: Sees all other proposals and responds/adjusts.

        basin_context (optional) carries the live scarcity signal so agents
        actually negotiate instead of restating positions:
            supply_maf       — water realistically available to the states
            total_rounds     — deadline (agreement must close by this round)
            last_claimed_maf — previous round's summed state claims (None in R1)
            last_deficit_maf — previous round's shortfall vs supply (None in R1)
        """
        pressure_text = self._build_pressure_text(round_number, basin_context)
        pressure_text += self._exhaustion_note()

        # A one-time correction if the moderator clamped our last proposal.
        feedback = self.pending_feedback
        feedback_block = f"{feedback}\n\n" if feedback else ""

        if round_number == 1:
            live_message = (
                f"{feedback_block}"
                f"Round {round_number} — Opening positions.\n"
                f"State your opening position as {self.mandate['full_name']}. "
                "What is your primary demand? What will you fight hardest to protect?"
                + pressure_text
            )
        else:
            other_text = json.dumps(
                [
                    {
                        "stakeholder":                p.get("state"),
                        "proposed_allocation_maf":    p.get("proposed_allocation_maf"),
                        "conservation_commitment_pct": p.get("conservation_commitment_pct"),
                        "shortage_sharing_position":  p.get("shortage_sharing_position"),
                        "key_demand":                 self._clip(p.get("key_demand")),
                        "concession_offered":         self._clip(p.get("concession_offered")),
                    }
                    for p in other_proposals
                ],
                indent=2,
            )
            live_message = (
                f"{feedback_block}"
                f"Round {round_number} — Responding to all stakeholders.\n\n"
                f"All proposals on the table:\n{other_text}\n"
                f"{pressure_text}\n"
                "How do you respond? Which positions align with yours? Which threaten "
                "your red lines? What position can you MOVE this round to help close the "
                "shortfall — and what do you ask for in return?"
            )

        # Two separate channels, deliberately kept apart so context can't grow:
        #   • live_message  — the FULL current table + pressure. Sent for THIS
        #     call only; never stored, so the bulky 12-peer table is not replayed
        #     every future round (that was blowing past --max-model-len).
        #   • memory_note   — a tiny placeholder stored in history to preserve
        #     user/assistant alternation. The agent's own trajectory is carried
        #     by its compact assistant replies; the current state of everyone
        #     else is always re-injected fresh via live_message.
        memory_note = f"[Round {round_number}] Reviewed the table and responded."

        # ── NEURAL: GPT generates the proposal ───────────────────────────────
        response = _get_client().chat.completions.create(
            model=_active_model,
            messages=[
                {"role": "system", "content": self._build_system_prompt()},
                *self.conversation_history,
                {"role": "user", "content": live_message},
            ],
            max_tokens=MAX_TOKENS_PER_CALL,
            temperature=0.7,
            response_format={"type": "json_object"},
        )

        raw_text = response.choices[0].message.content
        self.last_raw_generation = raw_text or ""

        try:
            proposal = json.loads(raw_text)
        except json.JSONDecodeError:
            print(f"  [WARNING] JSON parse error for {self.name} — using safe fallback.")
            proposal = {
                "state":                       self.name,
                "proposed_allocation_maf":     self.ceiling,
                "conservation_commitment_pct": 10,
                "shortage_sharing_position":   "moderate",
                "key_demand":                  f"Protect {self.mandate['full_name']} legal authority.",
                "concession_offered":          "Open to collaborative framework discussions.",
                "rationale":                   "Safe fallback due to JSON parse error.",
                "_parse_error":                True,
            }

        proposal["state"] = self.name   # Enforce correct identifier

        # Derived conviction (0–1) from observable behaviour — attached to the
        # proposal so the analysis layer can aggregate it across runs into the
        # rigidity map / confidence bands (see zopa_analyzer.py). Computed BEFORE
        # this round is appended to proposal_history, so it compares against the
        # previous round's values.
        proposal["conviction"] = self._compute_conviction(
            float(proposal.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0) or 0),
            float(proposal.get("conservation_commitment_pct", 0) or 0),
        )

        # Store a COMPACT reply in the LLM context (drop the verbose rationale,
        # clip text fields) so memory stays bounded round-over-round. The full
        # proposal is still kept in proposal_history (checkpoint/results) and the
        # full raw text in last_raw_generation (verbatim transcript log), so
        # nothing is lost from the outputs — only the replayed context shrinks.
        compact_reply = json.dumps({
            "proposed_allocation_maf":     proposal.get("proposed_allocation_maf"),
            "conservation_commitment_pct": proposal.get("conservation_commitment_pct"),
            "shortage_sharing_position":   proposal.get("shortage_sharing_position"),
            "key_demand":                  self._clip(proposal.get("key_demand")),
            "concession_offered":          self._clip(proposal.get("concession_offered")),
            "conviction":                  proposal.get("conviction"),
        })
        self.conversation_history.append({"role": "user",      "content": memory_note})
        self.conversation_history.append({"role": "assistant", "content": compact_reply})
        self._trim_history()

        # Correction (if any) has now been delivered — clear it so it fires once.
        self.pending_feedback = ""

        self.proposal_history.append({"round": round_number, "proposal": proposal})
        return proposal

    def note_constraint_correction(self, round_number: int, corrections: list[dict]) -> None:
        """
        Called by the moderator when one or more of this agent's proposal fields
        were clamped for breaching a hard limit (allocation ceiling, conservation
        cap, …). Does two things per field so the agent SELF-CORRECTS next round
        instead of re-anchoring on — and ratcheting up — the rejected number:
          1. Queues a plain correction note for the next prompt.
          2. Rewrites its most recent stored reply to the clamped (legal) values,
             so its own memory shows the corrected numbers, not the illegal ones.

        Each entry in `corrections` is a dict with:
          field, original, clamped, unit ("MAF"|"%"), noun, limit_word.
        """
        if not corrections:
            return

        lines = []
        for c in corrections:
            lines.append(
                f"Your round {round_number} {c['noun']} of "
                f"{float(c['original']):.3f}{c['unit']} breached your legal "
                f"{c['limit_word']} of {float(c['clamped']):.3f}{c['unit']} and was "
                f"automatically clamped to {float(c['clamped']):.3f}{c['unit']}. "
                f"Do not exceed {float(c['clamped']):.3f}{c['unit']} again."
            )
        self.pending_feedback = "CORRECTION — " + "  ".join(lines)

        # Rewrite the most recent stored assistant reply so memory shows the
        # legal values (all clamped fields at once, in a single scan).
        for msg in reversed(self.conversation_history):
            if msg.get("role") == "assistant":
                try:
                    d = json.loads(msg["content"])
                    for c in corrections:
                        if c["field"] in d:
                            d[c["field"]] = round(float(c["clamped"]), 3)
                    msg["content"] = json.dumps(d)
                except (json.JSONDecodeError, TypeError, ValueError):
                    pass
                break

    def reset(self):
        """Clears conversation and proposal history between simulation runs."""
        self.conversation_history = []
        self.proposal_history     = []

"""
negotiation.py — Mulholland Multiagent System
==============================================
NEGOTIATION ENGINE

Orchestrates one complete simulation run across all 13 agents:

  For each round (1 → NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM):

    # Category 1: Basin states (compact actors)

    "Colorado", "Utah", "Wyoming", "New Mexico", # Upper Basin
    # Category 2: Federal

def run_simulation(
    simulation_id: int,
    resume_state: dict | None = None,
    on_round_complete=None,
) -> dict:
    """
    Runs one complete Colorado River negotiation simulation across all 13 agents.

    Args:
        simulation_id : Integer identifier for this run (1–NUM_SIMULATIONS).
        resume_state  : Checkpoint state from a partially-completed run of this
                        same simulation_id (see checkpoint.py). Restores agent
                        memory and completed rounds, then continues from the
                        next unfinished round. None = start from round 1.
        on_round_complete : Optional callback invoked after each round completes,
                        as on_round_complete(simulation_id, round_num,
                        simulation_results, agent_states). main.py uses this to
                        write a checkpoint after every round.

    Returns:
        Dict containing rounds, final_proposals, constraint_violations,
        warnings, and basin_feasibility snapshots.
    """
    print(f"\n{'═'*66}")
    print(f"  SIMULATION {simulation_id:>2}  —  Colorado River Negotiation  (13 agents)")
    print(f"{'═'*66}")

    # Initialise all 13 agents fresh — clean history, no carryover between runs
    agents = {name: NegotiationAgent(name) for name in AGENT_ORDER}

    if resume_state:
        # Restore both halves of the state: the results accumulated so far, and
        # each agent's own memory. Restoring results without agent memory would
        # silently produce agents that had "forgotten" the rounds the results
        # say they participated in.
        simulation_results = resume_state["simulation_results"]
        for name, agent_state in resume_state.get("agent_states", {}).items():
            if name in agents:
                agents[name].load_state(agent_state)
        start_round = resume_state["next_round"]
        print(f"  ↻ Resuming from checkpoint — rounds 1–{start_round - 1} already complete.")
    else:
        simulation_results = {
            "simulation_id":         simulation_id,
            "rounds":                [],
            "constraint_violations": [],
            "warnings":              [],
            "basin_feasibility":     [],
            "final_proposals":       {},
        }
        start_round = 1

    # Previous round's basin totals — the live scarcity signal fed into each
    # agent's prompt so they negotiate toward closing the shortfall. On a resumed
    # run, seed it from the last completed round so pressure carries over.
    prev_basin = (
        simulation_results["basin_feasibility"][-1]
        if simulation_results["basin_feasibility"]
        else None
    )

    for round_num in range(start_round, NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM + 1):
        label = "(Opening positions)" if round_num == 1 else "(Response & adjustment)"
        print(f"\n  ── Round {round_num} of {NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM} {label} ──")

        # Scarcity/deadline context handed to every agent this round.
        basin_context = {
            "supply_maf":       AVAILABLE_SUPPLY_MAF,
            "total_rounds":     NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM,
            "last_claimed_maf": prev_basin["grand_total_maf"] if prev_basin else None,
            "last_deficit_maf": (
                round(prev_basin["grand_total_maf"] - AVAILABLE_SUPPLY_MAF, 3)
                if prev_basin else None
            ),
        }

        # Live transcript: banner for this round (see conversation_log.py)
        log_round_header(simulation_id, round_num, label)

        round_proposals  = []
        round_violations = []

        for agent_name, agent in agents.items():

            # Agent type label for console readability
            atype = agent.mandate["agent_type"].upper()[:4]
            print(f"    [{atype}] {agent_name:<20} → generating...", end=" ", flush=True)

            # ── NEURAL: GPT generates the proposal ──────────────────────────
            proposal = agent.generate_proposal(
                round_number    = round_num,
                other_proposals = round_proposals,  # All prior proposals visible
                basin_context   = basin_context,    # Live scarcity + deadline signal
            )

            # Applicable ceiling for this agent
            ceiling = ALL_AGENT_CEILINGS_MAF.get(agent_name, 0.0)

            # ── SYMBOLIC: Constraint checker validates ───────────────────────
            validation = check_proposal(proposal, round_proposals)

            if validation["valid"]:
                alloc = proposal.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0)
                print(
                    f"✓  {alloc:.3f} MAF  |  "
                    f"conservation: {proposal.get('conservation_commitment_pct', 0):.0f}%  |  "
                    f"shortage: {proposal.get('shortage_sharing_position', 'n/a')}"
                )
            else:
                print(f"✗  VIOLATION — clamping to {ceiling:.3f} MAF")
                for v in validation["violations"]:
                    print(f"           ⚠  {v}")
                    round_violations.append({
                        "agent": agent_name,
                        "round": round_num,
                        "violation": v,
                    })
                # Clamp every out-of-range field before accepting. Allocation is
                # capped at the legal ceiling; conservation is capped to 0–50%
                # (the implementable range enforced in constraint_checker.py).
                original_alloc = float(proposal.get("proposed_allocation_maf", ceiling))
                original_cons  = float(proposal.get("conservation_commitment_pct", 0))
                clamped_cons   = max(0.0, min(original_cons, 50.0))
                proposal["proposed_allocation_maf"]     = min(original_alloc, ceiling)
                proposal["conservation_commitment_pct"] = clamped_cons
                proposal["_constraint_clamped"] = True

                # Build the list of corrections to feed back so the agent
                # self-corrects next round instead of re-anchoring on — and
                # ratcheting up — the same illegal number.
                corrections = []
                if original_alloc > ceiling:
                    corrections.append({
                        "field": "proposed_allocation_maf",
                        "original": original_alloc, "clamped": round(ceiling, 3),
                        "unit": "MAF", "noun": "allocation", "limit_word": "ceiling",
                    })
                if not (0 <= original_cons <= 50):
                    corrections.append({
                        "field": "conservation_commitment_pct",
                        "original": original_cons, "clamped": clamped_cons,
                        "unit": "%", "noun": "conservation commitment", "limit_word": "cap",
                    })
                if corrections:
                    agent.note_constraint_correction(round_num, corrections)

            # Log non-blocking warnings
            for w in validation["warnings"]:
                print(f"           ℹ  {w}")
                simulation_results["warnings"].append({
                    "agent": agent_name, "round": round_num, "warning": w
                })

            # Live transcript: archive this agent's exact generated output plus
            # the constraint verdict, verbatim (see conversation_log.py).
            log_agent_turn(
                simulation_id  = simulation_id,
                round_number   = round_num,
                agent_name     = agent_name,
                agent_type     = agent.mandate["agent_type"],
                raw_generation = agent.last_raw_generation,
                validation     = validation,
            )

            round_proposals.append(proposal)

        # ── SYMBOLIC: Basin-level aggregate feasibility check ─────────────────
        basin_check          = check_basin_agreement(round_proposals)
        basin_check["round"] = round_num
        simulation_results["basin_feasibility"].append(basin_check)
        prev_basin           = basin_check   # feed this round's totals into the next

        feasible_flag = "✓" if basin_check["system_feasible"] else "✗"
        deficit_maf   = round(basin_check["grand_total_maf"] - AVAILABLE_SUPPLY_MAF, 3)
        gap_flag      = "GAP CLOSED ✓" if deficit_maf <= 0.05 else f"shortfall {deficit_maf:+.3f} MAF ✗"
        print(
            f"\n  Basin (compact states only) — "
            f"Upper: {basin_check['upper_basin_total_maf']} MAF  |  "
            f"Lower: {basin_check['lower_basin_total_maf']} MAF  |  "
            f"Grand: {basin_check['grand_total_maf']} MAF  {feasible_flag}"
        )
        print(
            f"  Scarcity — claimed {basin_check['grand_total_maf']} MAF vs "
            f"available {AVAILABLE_SUPPLY_MAF} MAF  →  {gap_flag}"
        )

        simulation_results["rounds"].append({
            "round_number": round_num,
            "proposals":    round_proposals,
            "violations":   round_violations,
            "basin_check":  basin_check,
        })
        simulation_results["constraint_violations"].extend(round_violations)

        # Persist progress. Done at round granularity (not per-agent) because a
        # round is the natural consistent unit — every agent has spoken and the
        # basin check has run, so a resumed run never starts mid-round with a
        # half-populated round_proposals list.
        if on_round_complete:
            on_round_complete(
                simulation_id,
                round_num,
                simulation_results,
                {name: agent.get_state() for name, agent in agents.items()},
            )

    # Final proposals = last round proposals keyed by agent name
    last_round = simulation_results["rounds"][-1]["proposals"]
    simulation_results["final_proposals"] = {p["state"]: p for p in last_round}

    print(f"\n  Final allocations (Round {NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM}):")
    for name, proposal in simulation_results["final_proposals"].items():
        atype = ALL_MANDATES[name]["agent_type"].upper()[:4]
        print(
            f"    [{atype}] {name:<20}: "
            f"{proposal.get('proposed_allocation_maf', 0):.3f} MAF"
            f"  — {proposal.get('key_demand', '')[:55]}"
        )

    violations_count = len(simulation_results["constraint_violations"])
    print(f"\n  Simulation {simulation_id} complete. Violations caught: {violations_count}")

    return simulation_results

def check_proposal(proposal: dict, all_current_proposals: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """
    Validates one agent's proposal against all symbolic legal constraints.

    Args:
        proposal             : The agent's output dict (parsed JSON from GPT).
        all_current_proposals: All proposals already accepted this round.
                               Used for cross-agent basin-level checks.
    Returns:
        valid      (bool) : True if no hard violations found.
        violations (list) : Hard breaches — block the proposal.
        warnings   (list) : Soft issues — logged but don't block.
    """
    violations = []
    warnings   = []

    agent      = proposal.get("state", "Unknown")
    allocation = float(proposal.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))

    # ── CONSTRAINT 1: Non-consumptive agents must propose 0.0 ────────────────
    # BOR and Environmental are legal/operational actors, not water claimants.
    if agent in NON_CONSUMPTIVE and allocation > 0.01:
        violations.append(
            f"NON-CONSUMPTIVE VIOLATION [{agent}]: Proposed {allocation:.3f} MAF "
            f"but this agent does not claim a water allocation (must be 0.0)."
        )
        return {"valid": False, "violations": violations, "warnings": warnings}

    # ── CONSTRAINT 2: Allocation ≤ applicable ceiling ────────────────────────
    # Uses ALL_AGENT_CEILINGS_MAF which covers both compact states and
    # non-state actors (tribal, treaty, agricultural).
    ceiling = ALL_AGENT_CEILINGS_MAF.get(agent, 0)
    if agent not in NON_CONSUMPTIVE and ceiling > 0:
        if allocation > ceiling * 1.05:       # 5% rounding tolerance
            violations.append(
                f"CEILING VIOLATION [{agent}]: Proposes {allocation:.3f} MAF "
                f"but ceiling is {ceiling:.3f} MAF "
                f"(excess: {allocation - ceiling:.3f} MAF)."
            )

    # ── CONSTRAINT 3: No negative allocations ────────────────────────────────
    if allocation < 0:
        violations.append(
            f"INVALID [{agent}]: Negative allocation ({allocation:.3f} MAF) proposed."
        )

    # ── CONSTRAINT 4: Conservation commitment must be realistic ──────────────
    conservation_pct = float(proposal.get("conservation_commitment_pct", 0))
    if not (0 <= conservation_pct <= 50):
        violations.append(
            f"INFEASIBLE [{agent}]: Conservation commitment {conservation_pct:.0f}% "
            f"is outside implementable range (0–50%)."
        )

    # ── CONSTRAINT 5: Basin-level totals (warning only — not a hard block) ───
    # Only applies to compact basin states; non-state agents don't count
    # toward compact basin totals.
    if agent in UPPER_BASIN_STATES or agent in LOWER_BASIN_STATES:
        if all_current_proposals:
            upper_total = sum(
                float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))
                for p in all_current_proposals
                if p.get("state") in UPPER_BASIN_STATES
            )
            lower_total = sum(
                float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))
                for p in all_current_proposals
                if p.get("state") in LOWER_BASIN_STATES
            )
            if agent in UPPER_BASIN_STATES:
                upper_total += allocation
            elif agent in LOWER_BASIN_STATES:
                lower_total += allocation

            if agent in UPPER_BASIN_STATES and upper_total > UPPER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF * 1.10:
                warnings.append(
                    f"BASIN WARNING: Upper Basin running total {upper_total:.2f} MAF "
                    f"exceeds compact ceiling {UPPER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF} MAF by >10%."
                )
            if agent in LOWER_BASIN_STATES and lower_total > LOWER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF * 1.10:
                warnings.append(
                    f"BASIN WARNING: Lower Basin running total {lower_total:.2f} MAF "
                    f"exceeds compact ceiling {LOWER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF} MAF by >10%."
                )

    # ── CONSTRAINT 6: Mexico treaty floor ────────────────────────────────────
    # If any agent explicitly references a Mexico delivery figure, validate it.
    mexico_alloc = proposal.get("mexico_allocation_maf")
    if mexico_alloc is not None:
        if float(mexico_alloc) < MEXICO_TREATY_SHORTAGE_MAF:
            violations.append(
                f"TREATY VIOLATION [{agent}]: Proposed Mexico delivery "
                f"{mexico_alloc} MAF is below treaty floor "
                f"{MEXICO_TREATY_SHORTAGE_MAF} MAF (Minute 323)."
            )

    return {
        "valid":      len(violations) == 0,
        "violations": violations,
        "warnings":   warnings,
    }


def check_basin_agreement(proposals: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """
    System-level feasibility check after all agents in a round have proposed.

    Only counts compact basin states toward basin totals —
    non-state actors (tribal, federal, etc.) are excluded from compact math.

    Returns basin totals and feasibility flags.
    """
    upper_total = sum(
        float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))
        for p in proposals if p.get("state") in UPPER_BASIN_STATES
    )
    lower_total = sum(
        float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))
        for p in proposals if p.get("state") in LOWER_BASIN_STATES
    )
    grand_total = upper_total + lower_total

    return {
        "upper_basin_total_maf":  round(upper_total, 3),
        "lower_basin_total_maf":  round(lower_total, 3),
        "grand_total_maf":        round(grand_total, 3),
        "upper_basin_feasible":   upper_total <= UPPER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF * 1.05,
        "lower_basin_feasible":   lower_total <= LOWER_BASIN_TOTAL_MAF * 1.05,
        "system_feasible":        grand_total <= 16.0,
    }

"""
zopa_analyzer.py — Mulholland Multiagent System
================================================
ZOPA ANALYZER — Zone of Possible Agreement

After all simulations complete, analyzes the full dataset to answer:

# Confidence bands (std dev, 95% CI, landing confidence) are only trustworthy
# once enough independent runs exist to show real spread. Below this, a single
# clustered result would masquerade as 100% confidence, so we report "n/a".
MIN_RUNS_FOR_CONFIDENCE = 20


def analyze_zopa(all_simulation_results: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """
    Full ZOPA analysis across all simulation runs.

    Args:
        all_simulation_results : List of dicts from run_simulation().

    Returns dict with:

    # ── Section 2: Stakeholder position summary (all 13 agents) ──────────────

    # ── Section 3: Coalition stability ────────────────────────────────────────

    # ── Section 5: Human-readable summary ────────────────────────────────────
    summary = _generate_summary(
        allocation_zopa, stakeholder_summary, coalition_stability, system_level

    return {

    }

def _compute_allocation_zopa(results: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """Allocation range analysis for consumptive agents (ceiling > 0)."""
    zopa = {}

    for agent in CONSUMPTIVE_AGENTS:
        final_allocs  = []
        round1_allocs = []
        convictions   = []

        for sim in results:
            final = sim["final_proposals"].get(agent, {})
            if final:
                final_allocs.append(float(final.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0)))
                if final.get("conviction") is not None:
                    convictions.append(float(final.get("conviction", 0)))
            for rd in sim["rounds"]:
                for p in rd["proposals"]:
                    if p.get("state") == agent and rd["round_number"] == 1:
                        round1_allocs.append(float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0)))

        if not final_allocs:
            continue

        ceiling    = ALL_AGENT_CEILINGS_MAF.get(agent, 1)
        n_runs     = len(final_allocs)
        final_mean = sum(final_allocs) / n_runs
        r1_mean    = (sum(round1_allocs) / len(round1_allocs)) if round1_allocs else final_mean

        # ── Dispersion across runs → statistical confidence ──────────────────
        # Std dev needs ≥2 samples; confidence bands need enough runs to mean
        # anything (see MIN_RUNS_FOR_CONFIDENCE) — otherwise report None ("n/a").
        std_dev = statistics.stdev(final_allocs) if n_runs >= 2 else 0.0
        enough  = n_runs >= MIN_RUNS_FOR_CONFIDENCE

        if enough:
            se_half = 1.96 * (std_dev / math.sqrt(n_runs))  # 95% CI half-width
            ci95 = {
                "low":  round(final_mean - se_half, 3),
                "high": round(final_mean + se_half, 3),
            }
            # How reliably the party lands in the same place: 1 = identical every
            # run, →0 = highly volatile. Normalised by the party's own ceiling.
            landing_confidence = round(max(0.0, 1.0 - (std_dev / ceiling)), 3) if ceiling > 0 else None
        else:
            ci95 = None
            landing_confidence = None

        mean_conviction = round(sum(convictions) / len(convictions), 3) if convictions else None

        zopa[agent] = {
            "ceiling_maf":             ceiling,
            "agent_type":              ALL_MANDATES[agent]["agent_type"],
            "n_runs":                  n_runs,
            "final_allocation_range":  {
                "min":  round(min(final_allocs), 3),
                "max":  round(max(final_allocs), 3),
                "mean": round(final_mean, 3),
            },
            "std_dev_maf":             round(std_dev, 3),
            "confidence_interval_95":  ci95,
            "landing_confidence":      landing_confidence,
            "mean_conviction":         mean_conviction,
            "avg_concession_maf":      round(ceiling - final_mean, 3),
            "flexibility_score":       round(
                (max(final_allocs) - min(final_allocs)) / ceiling, 4
            ) if ceiling > 0 else 0,
            "movement_toward_compromise_maf": round(r1_mean - final_mean, 3),
        }

    return zopa


def _compute_stakeholder_summary(results: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """Shortage stance and key demand summary for all 13 agents."""
    summary = {}

    for agent in ALL_MANDATES:
        stances   = []
        demands   = []

        for sim in results:
            final = sim["final_proposals"].get(agent, {})
            if final:
                if final.get("shortage_sharing_position"):
                    stances.append(final["shortage_sharing_position"])
                if final.get("key_demand"):
                    demands.append(final["key_demand"])

        if stances:
            # Most common stance across all simulation runs
            dominant_stance = max(set(stances), key=stances.count)
            summary[agent] = {
                "agent_type":      ALL_MANDATES[agent]["agent_type"],
                "dominant_shortage_stance": dominant_stance,
                "stance_consistency_pct":  round(stances.count(dominant_stance) / len(stances) * 100),
                "sample_key_demand":       demands[0] if demands else "",
            }

    return summary


def _compute_coalition_stability(results: list[dict]) -> list[dict]:
    """
    Coalition stability: how consistently two agents align on shortage stance.

    Stability = fraction of simulation runs where both agents share the
    same shortage_sharing_position in their final proposals.
    Only returns coalitions appearing in ≥60% of runs.
    """
    agents    = list(ALL_MANDATES.keys())
    n_sims    = len(results)
    coalitions = []

    for a, b in combinations(agents, 2):
        aligned = 0
        for sim in results:
            pa = sim["final_proposals"].get(a, {})
            pb = sim["final_proposals"].get(b, {})
            if (pa.get("shortage_sharing_position") and
                pb.get("shortage_sharing_position") and
                pa["shortage_sharing_position"] == pb["shortage_sharing_position"]):
                aligned += 1

        stability = aligned / n_sims if n_sims > 0 else 0
        if stability >= 0.6:
            coalitions.append({
                "agent_a":   a,
                "agent_b":   b,
                "stability": round(stability, 2),
                "label":     "HIGHLY STABLE" if stability >= 0.9 else
                             "STABLE"        if stability >= 0.75 else
                             "MODERATE",
            })

    return sorted(coalitions, key=lambda x: x["stability"], reverse=True)


def _compute_system_level(
    results: list[dict],
    allocation_zopa: dict,
    coalitions: list[dict]
) -> dict:
    """System-wide metrics: grand totals, rigid/flexible agents, verdict."""

    # Grand total = compact basin states only (non-state actors excluded)
    grand_totals = []
    for sim in results:
        total = sum(
            float(p.get("proposed_allocation_maf", 0))
            for p in sim["final_proposals"].values()
            if p.get("state") in STATE_AGENTS
        )
        grand_totals.append(total)

    mean_total = sum(grand_totals) / len(grand_totals) if grand_totals else 0

    # Feasibility confidence = share of runs whose final state claims actually
    # closed the shortfall (grand total within the available supply). This turns
    # the qualitative HIGH/MODERATE/LOW verdict into a measured probability.
    n_runs = len(grand_totals)
    feasible_runs = sum(1 for t in grand_totals if t <= AVAILABLE_SUPPLY_MAF + 0.05)
    feasibility_confidence = round(feasible_runs / n_runs, 3) if n_runs else None

    rigid_agents = [
        a for a, d in allocation_zopa.items()
        if d["flexibility_score"] < 0.05
    ]
    flexible_agents = sorted(
        allocation_zopa.items(),
        key=lambda x: x[1]["flexibility_score"],
        reverse=True
    )
    converging_agents = [
        a for a, d in allocation_zopa.items()
        if d["movement_toward_compromise_maf"] > 0.05
    ]

    total_violations = sum(len(s["constraint_violations"]) for s in results)

    if mean_total <= 14.5:
        feasibility = "HIGH — proposals consistently within drought-supply range"
    elif mean_total <= 16.0:
        feasibility = "MODERATE — proposals near compact ceiling; shortage-era gaps remain"
    else:
        feasibility = "LOW — total compact demands significantly exceed available supply"

    return {
        "compact_grand_total_maf": {
            "min":  round(min(grand_totals), 2) if grand_totals else 0,
            "max":  round(max(grand_totals), 2) if grand_totals else 0,
            "mean": round(mean_total, 2),
        },
        "total_constraint_violations": total_violations,
        "rigid_agents":      rigid_agents,
        "most_flexible":     [a for a, _ in flexible_agents[:3]],
        "converging_agents": converging_agents,
        "stable_coalitions": len([c for c in coalitions if c["stability"] >= 0.75]),
        "feasibility":       feasibility,
        "feasibility_confidence": feasibility_confidence,
        "n_runs":            n_runs,
    }

conversation_log.py — Mulholland Multiagent System
===================================================
LIVE TRANSCRIPT — every agent's raw generated output, every round, every run.

Writes output/all_conversations.txt as the simulation runs: for each agent, at
each round, of each simulation, the exact text the model generated — its stated
demand, concession, rationale, and shortage stance, verbatim. Read it after a
run to understand *why* coalitions formed, why proposals were rejected, or why
agents converged — the raw narrative behind the numeric ZOPA analysis.

Design decisions:
  • APPEND-ONLY. Every `python main.py` run adds a new, clearly delimited RUN
    section rather than overwriting — so the file is a running archive across
    all runs, exactly as requested ("for every run that we do").
  • WRITTEN PER TURN (open → append → close each call), so a crash, SLURM time
    limit, or node preemption never loses the transcript up to that point — the
    same durability philosophy as checkpoint.py. On resume, only the rounds that
    actually re-run get re-logged (already-completed rounds are skipped upstream).
  • NO-OP UNTIL INITIALISED. If init_conversation_log() was never called (e.g. a
    unit test importing negotiation.py directly), every log_* function silently
    does nothing — negotiation.py can call them unconditionally.

The record here is the raw model text. The structured/parsed proposals still
live in the results JSON; this file is the human-readable counterpart.
"""

import os
from datetime import datetime

# Single fixed file — one growing archive, not a per-run timestamped file.
DEFAULT_PATH = os.path.join("output", "all_conversations.txt")

_log_path = None


def init_conversation_log(path: str = DEFAULT_PATH, metadata: dict | None = None) -> None:
    """
    Enables logging and writes a RUN header. Call once at the start of a run
    (main.py). Uses append mode, so prior runs' transcripts are preserved.

    Args:
        path     : Output file. Defaults to output/all_conversations.txt.

    _log_path = path

    os.makedirs(directory, exist_ok=True)

    lines = [
        "",
        "=" * 80,
        f"RUN STARTED  {datetime.now().isoformat(timespec='seconds')}",
    ]
    if metadata:
        lines.append("  |  ".join(f"{k}: {v}" for k, v in metadata.items()))
    lines += ["=" * 80, ""]
    _append("\n".join(lines) + "\n")


def log_round_header(simulation_id: int, round_number: int, label: str = "") -> None:
    """Writes a banner marking the start of one round of one simulation."""
    if _log_path is None:
        return
    banner = f" SIMULATION {simulation_id}  |  ROUND {round_number} {label} ".strip()
    _append("\n" + "-" * 80 + "\n" + banner + "\n" + "-" * 80 + "\n")


def log_agent_turn(
    simulation_id: int,
    round_number: int,
    agent_name: str,
    agent_type: str,
    raw_generation: str,
    validation: dict | None = None,
) -> None:
    """
    Appends one agent's raw generated output for this round, followed by the
    symbolic constraint layer's verdict (so you can see, in place, whether that
    proposal was accepted as-is or clamped for crossing a legal ceiling).

    Args:
        raw_generation : The exact text the model produced (agent.last_raw_generation).
        validation     : The dict from constraint_checker.check_proposal(), or None.

        for w in validation.get("warnings", []):
            out += f"[constraint layer warning: {w}]\n"

    _append(out)

        f.write(text)
checkpoint.py
"""
checkpoint.py — Mulholland Multiagent System
=============================================
CRASH RECOVERY — save progress after every round, resume where it stopped.

A full run is NUM_SIMULATIONS × NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM × 13 agents of LLM calls
(3 × 100 × 13 = 3,900 at current settings — many hours). Anything that
interrupts it — a SLURM time limit, a preempted node, an OOM kill, a dropped
connection — otherwise throws away every completed round.

State is written after each round to output/checkpoint.json, so a re-run
picks up from the next unfinished round rather than starting over. The file
is deleted automatically once a run completes, so a normal finished run
leaves nothing behind to resume from.

Usage is driven from main.py:

    ckpt = load_checkpoint()          # None if no interrupted run exists
    ...
    save_checkpoint(state)            # after each round
    clear_checkpoint()                # after the whole run succeeds
"""

import json
import os
import tempfile

CHECKPOINT_PATH = os.path.join("output", "checkpoint.json")


def save_checkpoint(state: dict, path: str = CHECKPOINT_PATH) -> None:
    """
    Writes checkpoint state atomically: serialise to a temp file in the same
    directory, then os.replace() it over the target. os.replace is atomic on
    POSIX, so a crash *during* a write can never leave a half-written,
    unparseable checkpoint behind — you either get the previous good file or
    the new one, never a corrupt hybrid. This matters here precisely because
    the failures we're recovering from (SIGKILL, node preemption) can land
    mid-write.
    """
    directory = os.path.dirname(path) or "."
    os.makedirs(directory, exist_ok=True)

    fd, tmp_path = tempfile.mkstemp(dir=directory, suffix=".checkpoint.tmp")
    try:
        with os.fdopen(fd, "w") as f:
            json.dump(state, f, indent=2)
        os.replace(tmp_path, path)
    except BaseException:
        if os.path.exists(tmp_path):
            os.unlink(tmp_path)
        raise


def load_checkpoint(path: str = CHECKPOINT_PATH) -> dict | None:
    """
    Returns saved checkpoint state, or None if there's nothing to resume.

    A corrupt/unreadable checkpoint also returns None (with a warning) rather
    than raising — a bad checkpoint should cost you a restart, not block the
    run entirely.
    """
    if not os.path.exists(path):
        return None
    try:
        with open(path) as f:
            return json.load(f)
    except (json.JSONDecodeError, OSError) as e:
        print(f"  [WARNING] Checkpoint at {path} is unreadable ({e}) — starting fresh.")
        return None


def clear_checkpoint(path: str = CHECKPOINT_PATH) -> None:
    """Removes the checkpoint. Called once a full run finishes successfully."""
    if os.path.exists(path):
        os.remove(path)


def checkpoint_is_compatible(ckpt: dict, num_simulations: int, num_rounds: int) -> bool:
    """
    Guards against resuming into a config that no longer matches.

    If NUM_SIMULATIONS or NUM_ROUNDS_PER_SIM changed since the checkpoint was
    written, resuming would silently mix results produced under two different
    experiment configurations into one output file — which is worse than
    losing the progress, because the corruption is invisible in the results.
    """
    saved = ckpt.get("config", {})
    return (
        saved.get("num_simulations") == num_simulations
        and saved.get("num_rounds_per_sim") == num_rounds
    )

Methods, simplifications, and AI disclosure

Implemented from the published Model Guide

  • Accounts: Reclamation, California, Arizona, Nevada, Mexico, Tribal Nations of the Lower Basin (Guide Step 1).
  • Evaporation: 6.0 ft/yr × lake surface area at current storage, split among accounts in proportion to balances (Guide 1B.i, 3B). Surface area and storage↔elevation use the official USBR Lake Mead area-capacity tables (2001/2009 survey) — the same family of data the Guide's spreadsheet uses. Cross-check: at 1,055 ft the tables give 74,000 acres → 0.444 maf/yr, matching the Guide's worked example of 0.44 maf.
  • Inflow division: Reclamation first receives a block offsetting its evaporation share, which holds the protection volume constant while inflow covers the offset (Guide Step 4); below that, the protection volume erodes — the model guarantees no floor. The remainder is divided by the Guide's Table 4a default: Tribal Nations take 0.95 − 0.106 × shortage off the top (Ten Tribes Partnership settled rights); states take historical allocation minus their 2024 Lower Basin Alternative share of the shortage (AZ 43%, CA 36.7%, Mexico 16.7%, NV 3.6% of the total shortage) minus their locational share of tribal water (AZ 82.2%, CA 16.4%, NV 1.3%). Verified against the Guide's own worked example at 6.3 maf inflow (CA 3.30, AZ 1.08, Mexico 1.05, Tribal 0.67).
  • Dashboards: one-year buy/sell trades at an agreed $/af with compensation computed (Guide Step 5); net trades must sum to zero; withdrawals limited to available water; balances carry over year to year (Steps 5–7).
  • Mass balance is exact every year: end-of-year lake storage = start + inflow − evaporation − total withdrawals. Raw user shares from the Guide's rounded coefficients (the tribal location percentages sum to 99.9%) exceed the entered inflow by 0.011% throughout the shortage regime; shares are normalized to the divisible inflow so water balances exactly. Above the 9.0 maf full-allocation basis the Guide's table has no shortage row, so shares are scaled proportionally — an extension beyond the documented rules.

Documented simplifications and omissions

  • This is an adaptation of the published model, not the authors' spreadsheet. The mandatory-conservation cutback schedule and real ICS account balances live in the authors' Google Sheets/Excel file, which is not in the public repository; both are omitted. Shortage sharing enters through the inflow split above instead.
  • The Step 3 initial storage split is a negotiated joint choice in the published model; here it defaults to the Guide's pro-rata suggestion.
  • Protection elevation is fixed at session start (1,020 or 1,000 ft). Mid-session changes are possible in the full model.
  • Only two price points are documented in the Guide: about $400/af (500-Plus Plan, $200M for 0.5 maf/yr) and the $500/af worked trade example. Any price entered in play is the player's or AI's own choice within the 0–10,000 $/af bound — an illustration of the mechanism, not a documented or predicted price.
  • The Participant dashboard tab mirrors the per-account spreadsheet layout from David Rosenberg's session walkthrough (salmon input cells; starting available water, compensation, net-trade tracker, available water, and end-of-year balance computed live). Differences from his sheet, by design: price is entered unsigned with compensation signed by direction (his example slide signs the price cell and its California row contradicts its own annotated formula Compensation = Price × Volume); matched trades must agree on one price (his sheet checks only that volumes net to zero); each account enters one net volume rather than per-trade columns; and Reclamation's protection volume stays fixed rather than being played.
  • Role briefs, hard lines, and would-trade positions (shown in the player panels and the Live AI bridge prompt) are built from cited public sources on the real parties' documented positions, detailed in the author's bibliography. Where a game rule goes beyond what a source states, the text marks it as the author's inference. Every quantity on this page comes from the Model Guide (4.4 / 2.8 / 0.3 / 1.5 / 0.95 MAF allocations, Table 1a/4a shortage shares, 0.47 of 0.95 MAF tribal settled rights currently used - Ten Tribes Partnership 2018). The author's first-draft scenario positions and their figures (his April 2026 design) were removed at the author's direction in November 2026 and are not used anywhere on this page.
  • Hard-line checks flag but never block: when a settled move crosses a player's stated hard line, the year result and the history table mark it. The Positions and stated reasons table doubles as the negotiation log from the author's Rule 4(f) game design (positions, concessions, stated reasons, hard-line crossings, per year) and exports as JSON via "Copy session log".
  • What a session can and cannot show: it shows where each played position moved year to year (withdrawals, trades, prices), what the players said drove each move, and where hard lines held or broke - raw material for discussing a zone of possible agreement. It does not show what real states, Mexico, or Tribal Nations would do: roles are played by people or by one AI conversation from simplified sourced briefs, and AI rationales are self-reported, not observed motives.
